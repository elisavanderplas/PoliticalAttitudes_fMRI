Threat and Blame Frames in Political Rhetoric: Neural and Political Polarization

Code and materials for:

Van der Plas, E., Todorova, L., Heidlmayr, K., Jansen, G., Rosema, M., & Sanfey, A. G. (2026). Threat and blame frames in political rhetoric about societal issues lead to neural and political polarization. Scientific Reports, 16, 14304. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-43389-9

Overview

This project examines how online political content that frames societal issues (climate change, immigration, healthcare) in terms of threat or blame affects voting behaviour and brain activity, compared to neutral framing.

Two studies are reported:

Study 1 — a large-scale online experiment (N = 1,825 Dutch adults) testing the effect of threat/blame framing on self-reported emotion, issue importance, video-clip sharing intentions, and political party attitudes.
Study 2 — a within-subject fMRI study (N = 27) replicating the behavioural findings and using inter-subject correlation (ISC) analysis to test whether threat/blame framing changes how consistently the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) is engaged across participants.
Repository contents

data/ — study data supporting the analyses.
